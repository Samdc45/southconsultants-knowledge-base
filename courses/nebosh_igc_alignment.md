## What is NEBOSH IGC?

The **NEBOSH International General Certificate in Occupational Health and Safety** is the gold-standard entry-level H&S qualification recognised globally — Middle East, SE Asia, Africa, UK, NZ/AU, Canada, US.

**2025/2026 Update:** The syllabus was revised in 2025. From 2 February 2026, the new spec uses unit codes **GIC1** (was IG1) and **GIC2** (was IG2). Both old and new specs run in parallel until final IG1 assessment 5 August 2026.

**Structure:**
- **Unit GIC1/IG1** — Management of Health and Safety (Open Book Exam, 24-hour window, 4-5 hours to complete)
- **Unit GIC2/IG2** — Risk Assessment (Practical, 3 hours, workplace-based)

**Pass mark:** 45% (IG1). Grade boundaries: Pass 45–64, Credit 65–74, Distinction 75+.  
**Duration:** ~63 taught hours + 40 hours private study.  
**Cost:** Set by Learning Partners (typically USD $500–$1,500 depending on region/format).  
**Languages:** English, Arabic, French, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Turkish.

**Official resource:** https://www.nebosh.org.uk/qualifications/international-general-certificate/  
**Full syllabus (2018 spec LP guide):** https://www.nebosh.org.uk/documents/ig-lp-syllabus-guide/igc-lp-guide-v3.2.pdf  
**New 2025 spec learner guide:** https://www.nebosh.org.uk/documents/gic-syllabus-guide-for-learners/gic-learner-guide-v1.pdf

## Full NEBOSH IGC Syllabus

### Unit IG1/GIC1: Management of Health and Safety

**Element 1: Why we should manage workplace H&S**
- 1.1 Moral and financial reasons for H&S (insured vs uninsured costs)
- 1.2 Regulation: enforcement agencies, ISO 45001, ILO C155 / R164 (employer/worker duties)
- 1.3 Roles in organisations: directors, managers, supervisors; contractor selection, monitoring, management

**Element 2: How H&S management systems work**
- 2.1 The Plan-Do-Check-Act model (ISO 45001:2018, ILO-OSH 2001); benefits of formal/certified systems
- 2.2 Policy, responsibilities, practical arrangements; when to review the system

**Element 3: Managing risk — understanding people and processes**
- 3.1 H&S culture — definition, relationship to performance, leading indicators
- 3.2 Improving H&S culture — management commitment, leadership, training (induction/job change/technology), consultation/committees
- 3.3 Human factors — organisational, job, and individual factors; behaviour
- 3.4 Risk assessment 5-step process — identify hazards, people at risk, evaluate/control, record, review; hierarchy of controls (ISO 45001:2018 Clause 8.1.2)
- 3.5 Management of change — construction works, process/equipment change, segregation, emergency procedure amendments
- 3.6 Safe systems of work — task analysis, procedural/technical/behavioural controls
- 3.7 Permit-to-work — when to use (hot work, electrical isolation, confined spaces, WAH, machinery maintenance)
- 3.8 Emergency procedures — what to include, testing, first aid needs assessment

**Element 4: Health and safety monitoring and measuring**
- 4.1 Active monitoring (inspections, sampling, tours) vs reactive monitoring (incidents, near misses, ill-health data); leading/lagging indicators
- 4.2 Incident investigation — 4 steps; recording and notification (ILO Code of Practice)
- 4.3 Auditing — definition, purpose, types, stages
- 4.4 Performance review — what to review, continuous improvement loop

---

### Unit IG2/GIC2: Risk Assessment (Practical)

> Learners carry out a risk assessment of their own workplace, covering hazards from Elements 5–11.  
> Must identify 10 hazards from at least 5 different hazard categories.

**Element 5: Physical and psychological health**
- 5.1 Noise — health effects, dB(A)/dB(C), exposure limits, noise controls, hearing protection, health surveillance
- 5.2 Vibration — HAV and WBV effects, exposure limits, controls (low-vibration tools, rotas, PPE)
- 5.3 Radiation — non-ionising and ionising, health effects, sources, controls, health surveillance
- 5.4 Mental ill-health — depression, anxiety, PTSD; HSE Management Standards (demands, control, support, relationships, role, change)
- 5.5 Violence at work — types (physical, psychological, bullying); high-risk roles; controls
- 5.6 Substance abuse — alcohol, drugs, solvents; controls

**Element 6: Musculoskeletal health**
- 6.1 Work-related upper limb disorders (WRULDs) — repetitive strain, workstation ergonomics
- 6.2 Manual handling — injury types, good technique, TILE assessment (Task, Individual, Load, Environment)
- 6.3 Load-handling equipment — sack trucks, forklifts, hoists, cranes; safe lifting operations requirements; inspection/examination

**Element 7: Chemical and biological agents**
- 7.1 Hazardous substances — dusts, fumes, gases, liquids; fungi, bacteria, viruses; acute vs chronic
- 7.2 Health risk assessment — routes of entry; safety data sheets; exposure monitoring
- 7.3 Occupational exposure limits — TWA, STEL, comparison to standards
- 7.4 Control measures — hierarchy: eliminate, substitute, enclose, ventilate, PPE
- 7.5 Specific agents — asbestos, blood-borne viruses, carbon monoxide, cement, Legionella, Leptospira, silica, wood dust

**Element 8: General workplace issues**
- 8.1 Health, welfare and environment — drinking water, sanitation, rest areas, temperature extremes
- 8.2 Working at height — risk factors; hierarchy of controls (avoid, prevent, minimise); ladders, scaffolds, MEWPs, fall-arrest; emergency rescue
- 8.3 Confined spaces — types, hazards (atmospheric), PTW requirements, rescue planning
- 8.4 Lone working — hazards, controls, risk assessment
- 8.5 Slips and trips — causes, controls (surfaces, housekeeping)
- 8.6 Vehicle and pedestrian movement — segregation, site access, driver training, banksman/reversing
- 8.7 Work-related driving — safe driver/vehicle/journey; fleet management; electric/hybrid vehicle hazards

**Element 9: Work equipment**
- 9.1 General requirements — suitable equipment, preventing access to dangerous parts, maintenance, markings
- 9.2 Hand-held tools — selection, condition, suitability, hazards and controls
- 9.3 Machinery hazards — grinders, drills, cement mixers, chainsaws, circular saws, drones, driverless vehicles
- 9.4 Control measures — guards (fixed, interlocking, adjustable), trip devices, emergency stops, PPE

**Element 10: Fire**
- 10.1 Fire principles — fire triangle, classification, heat transmission, causes
- 10.2 Preventing fire — reduce fuels, control ignition, good housekeeping, structural measures (compartmentation, fire doors)
- 10.3 Alarms and fire-fighting — detection systems, extinguisher types/media, fire service access
- 10.4 Evacuation — escape routes, emergency lighting, fire marshals, roll call, disabled persons

**Element 11: Electricity**
- 11.1 Hazards — electric shock (severity factors), electrical fires, portable equipment, overhead lines, underground cables
- 11.2 Controls — conductor protection, fuses, earthing, RCDs, isolation, competent persons, safe systems of work, PAT testing, inspection records

## Gap-Fill Roadmap to Achieve ~75% NEBOSH Alignment

### Priority 1 — Fill empty shells (immediately unlocks IG1 coverage)
1. **Build Safety Leadership courses (21 & 26)** → covers IG1 Elements 2, 3, 4 entirely (management systems, culture, monitoring, incident investigation, auditing)

### Priority 2 — New courses (unlock IG2 coverage)
2. **Build Working at Heights course** → covers IG2 Element 8.2 (currently 0%)
3. **Build Confined Space course** → covers IG2 Element 8.3 (currently 0%)
4. **Build Hazardous Substances / COSHH module** → covers IG2 Element 7 (cement dust, silica, asbestos — critical for civil construction)
5. **Build Fire Safety module** → covers IG2 Element 10 (currently 0%)

### Priority 3 — Module additions to existing courses
6. Add **manual handling / TILE assessment** module to Induction or Safety Fundamentals → covers IG2 Element 6.2
7. Add **noise and vibration** health effects module to plant/compaction courses → covers IG2 Elements 5.1/5.2
8. Add **substance abuse and mental health** module to Safety Leadership → covers IG2 Elements 5.4/5.6
9. Add **electrical safety** general module (not just underground cables) → covers IG2 Element 11

## Strategic Opportunity: NEBOSH Learning Partner

SC could apply to become a NEBOSH **Accredited Learning Partner**, allowing it to:
- Deliver NEBOSH IGC officially in NZ, AU, and (with US/EU offices) globally
- Issue NEBOSH-endorsed certificates to learners
- Charge premium fees (NEBOSH IGC courses typically $500–$1,500 per learner)
- Differentiate from Site Safe NZ / CHASNZ (neither is a NEBOSH LP focused on civil construction)

**Requirements to become a Learning Partner:**
- Demonstrate qualified tutors (IIRSM/IOSH membership, relevant industry experience)
- Submit accreditation application to NEBOSH (Leicester, UK)
- Pass course quality review
- Submit proposed timetables and course structure for approval

**Contact:** NEBOSH LP Programme — https://www.nebosh.org.uk/guidance/guidance-for-learning-partners/
